Maths chapters

The wrong answer is the useful bit

In maths, which wrong answer a child gives tells you the misconception. 320 for 34 of 240 means they divided by 3 and multiplied by 4. So every question carries its known wrong answers mapped to what went wrong, and the feedback names the mistake instead of saying "incorrect". Type 320 and see.

Type it, don't pick it

A number pad, not A–D. Multiple choice lets a child guess right while understanding nothing, and it hides the misconception you want to catch. Exam style exists in the rig for paper familiarity — and note where its distractors come from: the same traps map. A child who picks the trap gets the same named explanation either way.

Anything not in traps is logged, not shrugged at

A generic "let's walk through it" is the fallback, never the plan. Every unlisted wrong answer lands in the log at the bottom of this page with a count against it. A value that keeps recurring is a misconception nobody wrote down yet — that log is how the trap lists get good, and reviewing it is a content job, not a code one.

Show the working, then make them redo it

Steps reveal one at a time, then the same question comes back with the steps hidden. Seeing a worked solution teaches far less than re-walking it. A learner who already spots their error can take Skip — I see it and lose nothing.

One transferable line per question

"Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top." "Perimeter is the walk around the edge; area is the grass inside." The rule is what carries to the next question — the answer doesn't. It is shown on a correct answer and again at the end of the working.

A model wherever one exists

Fraction bars, number lines, arrays and labelled shapes, all SVG. For Grades 4–7 the picture often is the explanation, so a question that has a model renders it in the stem, not in the answer.

Chapters gate on the prerequisite

The ladder is ordered by dependency, not by syllabus number: bonds and tables → the four operations → fractions → decimals and percentages → ratio → measurement → money → geometry → data → early algebra. A locked chapter names its blocker. Pick a locked one in the rig and the round refuses to start.

No clock, anywhere

Fluency is measured by streak and by first-time-correct rate — including on times tables. A countdown filters out exactly the learners who need the practice: slower readers, cheap phones with laggy touch, and anyone who stops to think.
