Other kinds of cut

Why chunking is the right fix

Nobody spells necessary by remembering nine letters in a row — they hold three or four chunks. Teaching the cut turns an unspellable word into a small, ordered set the child can carry. It also transfers: the cut that fixes separate fixes desperate and celebrate too, which is what the last screen names.

Show, then make them do it, then take the help away

Three steps in this order. The reveal alone teaches almost nothing; the rebuild while the shape is fresh is what does the work; and then the word joins the tricky pool and comes back later with no chunking shown at all. Scaffolding that never fades isn't teaching.

The cut depends on the word, not always syllables

Five strategies — syllables, root+affix, trap, sound-out, family — and younger bands get onset–rime (str | eet) rather than syllables. Switch word in the strip above to see each one; the second phone lists them side by side.

Chunks are content, not code

Every word needs a hand-checked chunks, a trap index and a strategy. Generated syllabification is wrong often enough to teach children the wrong break — and a bad memory hook is worse than none. This needs the same approval gate as your notes. The badge above runs the one check that can be automated: chunks.join('') === word, validated on save. Press Break the schema on purpose to watch it fail.

Hooks are optional and rare

"There's a rat in separate" works because it's true and memorable. Most words don't have one — pick receive or street in the strip and note that no empty gold box is rendered. Leave the field empty rather than inventing something forgettable.

Let them skip

A child who already knows what they did wrong gets "Skip — I've got it" on the first screen. The word still joins their tricky pool; the lesson is never forced.
