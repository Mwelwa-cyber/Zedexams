Why there's no timer

For spelling, speed is not the skill — accuracy is. A countdown punishes exactly the learners who most need the practice: slower readers, children on cheap phones with laggy touch, anyone who stops to think. They get the worst experience and quit first. Tension here comes from the streak and finishing the set, not a clock. This is the default for every learning game in the engine; a timed mode, if ever wanted, is a separate mode the learner opts into — never the default and never the only option.

Build, don't type

Letter tiles instead of a keyboard. The child still produces the word — which is what makes spelling stick — but a fat-finger typo can't be mistaken for a spelling error, and it works one-handed on a small screen. Tiles are the word's own letters plus decoys, and the decoy count scales by grade: about 2 at Grade 4, about 5 by Grade 9. Change the grade above and watch the tile row.

Hear it first, and never block on audio

The word is spoken and not shown — a sentence with the gap gives context. Listening then producing is the strongest form of the exercise; showing the word turns it into copying. Press Simulate no audio to see the fallback: the word flashes for two seconds and then hides. A round never stops because a voice failed to load.

Wrong answers aren't punished

No lives, no buzzer, no lost points. A miss shows the correct spelling with the wrong letters marked, then that word is requeued three positions later in the same round. Getting it right on the second pass is the whole point of the exercise.

Hints are free

A hint places the next correct letter. It's recorded so "first-time correct" stays honest, and it deducts nothing and breaks nothing. Score is first-time-correct percentage, best streak and words mastered — no time bonus anywhere.

Tricky words are the real feature

Every missed word joins the learner's persistent pool and resurfaces in later rounds on a spaced schedule, mixed in with new words. This prototype keeps that pool in localStorage so you can finish a round, reload the page, and watch the same words come back. A spelling game without this is a quiz; with it, it teaches.

Per-word attempts are recorded

Every attempt logs {word, attempts, hinted, correct}. That is what feeds My Progress, the study plan and the guardian's weekly report — the end screen prints the raw log so you can see exactly what the app would write.

British spelling

ECZ marks British forms — colour, realise, neighbour, favourite, and practise (verb) vs practice (noun). The badge above runs a real check over the word list on load. Pin this in the content rules: generated word lists drift American by default.

Works offline

The round's word list and its audio cache as a unit, so a learner with no data can still play. Every rule above runs with the network off in this prototype — the one request it makes is the web font, which falls back to the system stack, and in the app the font ships in the bundle.
