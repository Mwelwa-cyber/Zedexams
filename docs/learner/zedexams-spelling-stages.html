A path, not a playlist

Chapters group words by what makes them hard, not by topic — double letters, silent letters, -ie/-ei, suffix rules, homophones. That's a real difficulty ladder a child can feel climbing, and it's how spelling is actually taught. Nine chapters of 10–12 stages, eight words a stage.

This is the answer to "the same six words again"

Every stage is composed at run time from (bank, this learner's pool, stage number): about 6 new words + up to 3 of their own missed words that are due. Switch learner in the strip above and open the same stage — different eight words. Press Replay on a cleared stage and it reshuffles from the same difficulty band rather than serving the identical list.

The gold node is theirs alone

A Tricky words stage appears whenever a learner has 10+ unmastered words, and it sits between the numbered ones. Nobody else's map has it in the same place. Press Seed 11 tricky words to make it appear.

Mastery is three rights in three separate sessions

Three in one sitting is short-term memory. The stage-complete screen shows the countdown per word, and only the third separate session removes a word from the pool. The pool is the same data My Progress, the study plan and the guardian's weekly report read — there is no second tracker.

Stars are accuracy only, never speed

3★ = all 8 right first time, 2★ = 6–7, 1★ = finished. Hinted words don't count as first-time correct. Cleared stages stay open and replayable, and a replay can raise the star count but never lower it — try replaying a 3★ stage badly.

Never block a learner

Clearing a stage unlocks the next, but after two unsuccessful attempts the stage offers a shorter practice round on just those words and then lets them through. No lives, no energy meter, no wait-to-continue, no paid retries, no "buy more turns". Borrow the map, the stars and the pull to the next stage from games like Candy Crush — leave every monetisation mechanic out. A child must never be stopped from practising.

Content is the real constraint, not the code

At 8 words a stage, ~100 stages per grade band needs roughly 800 approved words per grade, each with a difficulty band, a context sentence and audio. The dark panel on the map screen reports what actually exists per grade before the map is built, so the gap is visible rather than discovered at stage 30. Stage composition is a pure function of (bank, pool, stage number), so stages are generated rather than hand-authored.

Offline

A whole chapter downloads as a unit — words, sentences and audio — and progress syncs when the learner is back online.
