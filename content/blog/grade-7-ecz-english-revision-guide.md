English is the paper Grade 7 learners most often under-revise — it
feels like something you either "can or can't do". That's wrong.
Comprehension, grammar and composition are all trainable skills, and
the ECZ paper tests the same families of question year after year.
This guide breaks the syllabus into a six-week plan, the same shape as
our [Grade 7 Mathematics guide](/blog/grade-7-ecz-maths-revision-guide).

## The six-week shape

| Week | Focus area | Why it goes here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grammar and structure | Tenses, subject–verb agreement, plurals — the highest-frequency marks |
| 2 | Vocabulary in context | Synonyms, opposites, choosing the right word in a sentence |
| 3 | Comprehension technique | Finding answers in the passage instead of guessing from memory |
| 4 | Punctuation and sentence building | Capital letters, full stops, question marks, joining sentences |
| 5 | Composition practice | Short guided writing — order, linking words, checking your work |
| 6 | Past papers + timed mocks | Pull it all together under exam conditions |

Aim for **four 25-minute sessions a week**: two on the week's new
focus, one re-attempting last week's wrong answers, one timed sprint.

## The comprehension habit that earns the most marks

Almost every comprehension mark is *in the passage*. The routine:

1. Read the questions **first**, so you know what to hunt for.
2. Read the passage once at normal speed.
3. For each question, find and underline the exact sentence the answer
   lives in before you write anything.
4. Answer in a full sentence, using your own words where the question
   asks for meaning.

Learners who guess from memory after one read lose easy marks to
learners who go back to the text every single time.

## Grammar topics that come up almost every year

- Present, past and future tense — and keeping one tense through a sentence
- Subject–verb agreement ("the dogs *run*", "the dog *runs*")
- Singular and plural forms, including the irregular ones
- Opposites and synonyms
- Prepositions (in, on, at, under, between)
- Direct punctuation — capitals, full stops, commas in lists

## Composition: the checklist beats inspiration

Strong Grade 7 compositions aren't the most imaginative ones — they're
the *clearest* ones. Before you call a practice composition done,
check:

- Does every sentence start with a capital and end with a full stop?
- Did I stay in the same tense from start to finish?
- Did I use at least three linking words (first, then, after that, finally)?
- Did I read it aloud once to catch missing words?

Do one short composition a week from week 3, and re-read the previous
one before writing the next. The improvement curve is visible within
a month.

## The final two weeks

Switch to full past papers using the three-pass method from our
[past-papers guide](/blog/ecz-grade-7-past-papers-guide) — timed
attempt, open-book repair, closed-book confirmation. English rewards
this more than any other subject, because the question *styles* repeat
even when the passages change.
