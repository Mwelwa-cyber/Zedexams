Past papers are the single highest-value revision resource for the
Grade 7 ECZ examinations — no textbook chapter predicts the real paper
better than the real papers that came before it. But most learners use
them badly: they read through a paper once, nod along at the answers,
and move on. This guide covers where to find Grade 7 past papers and
the routine that actually converts them into marks.

## The three-pass method

One paper, three passes, spread across a week. This beats attempting
three different papers once each.

| Pass | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Timed | Day 1 | Full paper, exam conditions, no notes. Mark it honestly. |
| 2 — Repair | Day 3 | Redo ONLY the questions you lost marks on, open-book. Write down *why* each one went wrong. |
| 3 — Confirm | Day 7 | Redo the repaired questions again, closed-book. Anything still wrong goes on your weak-topic list. |

The second pass is where the learning happens. The third pass is how
you check the learning stuck.

## Reading your mistakes like an examiner

When you mark pass 1, sort every lost mark into one of three buckets:

- **Knowledge gaps** — you didn't know the content. Fix with topic
  revision and quizzes, not more past papers.
- **Method slips** — you knew it but set the working out wrongly or
  skipped steps. Fix by writing full working on every practice question
  for two weeks, even the easy ones.
- **Exam-craft errors** — misread the question, ran out of time, left
  blanks. Fix with timed sprints and a fixed time-per-question budget.

Most learners assume every mistake is a knowledge gap and re-read
textbooks. In the marking we see, method slips and exam-craft errors
usually cost more marks than missing knowledge.

## How many papers is enough?

Quality beats volume. Four to six papers per subject, each given the
full three-pass treatment, outperforms racing through ten papers once.
Start six to eight weeks before the examination sits — pairing this
with the [week-by-week revision routine](/blog/how-to-revise-week-by-week)
gives you the schedule to hang it on.
