If your child's school talks about "CBC", "competencies" or
"learning outcomes" and it sounds different from the school you
remember — this guide is for you. Here's what Zambia's
Competency-Based Curriculum actually changes, in plain language, and
what it means for homework, tests and how you can help at home.

## What CBC is, in one paragraph

The Competency-Based Curriculum shifts the question from *"what facts
has the child memorised?"* to *"what can the child actually do with
what they've learned?"*. Instead of measuring progress mainly by
recall, each subject is organised around **competencies** — skills
like solving a real-world measurement problem, writing a clear
paragraph, or interpreting a simple chart — with learning outcomes
that describe what a learner should be able to demonstrate at each
grade.

## What changes for your child day to day

- **More applied questions.** Maths word problems about market prices
  and travel times, rather than bare sums; English tasks built around
  reading and writing for a purpose.
- **Continuous assessment matters.** Classwork, projects and
  school-based assessment count alongside end-of-term tests, so steady
  effort through the term is rewarded — not just exam-week cramming.
- **Examinations still exist.** The Examinations Council of Zambia
  (ECZ) still examines learners — Grade 7 remains a key examination
  year — but the questions increasingly reward understanding and
  application over pure recall.

## What stays the same

The subjects your child studies, the school calendar, and the role of
the class teacher don't change. CBC is a change in *how* content is
taught and assessed, not a new set of subjects. A child who practises
regularly and gets quick feedback still comes out ahead — that part
has never changed.

## How to support CBC learning at home

1. **Ask "show me how", not "what did you get".** Competency is
   demonstrated, so have your child explain their working on one
   homework question a day. Explaining is practice.
2. **Little and often beats marathon sessions.** Twenty focused
   minutes a day builds the steady record that continuous assessment
   rewards. Our [week-by-week revision routine](/blog/how-to-revise-week-by-week)
   is built around exactly this.
3. **Use practice that gives instant feedback.** ZedExams quizzes are
   CBC-aligned for Grades 4–7 and mark themselves immediately, so a
   wrong answer becomes a lesson while the question is still fresh.
   Learning [games](/games) do the same job for younger learners
   without it feeling like homework.
4. **For Grade 7 parents:** start [past-paper practice](/papers) six
   to eight weeks before the examinations, using the three-pass method
   in our [past-papers guide](/blog/ecz-grade-7-past-papers-guide).

## The one-sentence summary

CBC asks your child to *use* what they learn — and the best support at
home is short, regular practice with fast feedback, plus the patience
to let them explain their thinking out loud.
